# OPIc AL Strategy Guide (문법·전략 총정리)

Target: **AL (Advanced Low)**. This guide combines personal notes with the ACTFL Advanced-Low
descriptor, organized around four grammar priorities — **transitions → tense → plurals → articles** —
plus answer playbooks per question type, phrase banks, and topic cards. Drill the structures; generate
the content live from the question (rehearsed-sounding answers are capped by the raters).

## 1. How OPIc scoring works (IM → AL)

- ~40-minute recorded computer interview; questions come from your **Background Survey**.
- Levels: IL → IM → **IH → AL** → AM. The IH→AL jump is the goal.
- **The AL descriptor, concretely**: narrate/describe in **all major time frames** (past · present ·
  future) in **paragraph-length discourse**; **connect sentences with appropriate conjunctions so the
  link between them is strong**; handle a **complication** (unexpected turn) in a familiar situation;
  use **varied adjectives**.
- **"Errors do not interfere"** = patterns matter, not slips. One dropped plural **-s** is nothing;
  dropping it on every countable noun is a **pattern**, and patterns block AL.
- **Timing**: Description/Habit ~1 minute (low weight); **PE / Comparison up to 1:30 (high weight)**.
- What each level sounds like:

| Level | What the rater hears |
|---|---|
| IL | Short sentences, one at a time. Present tense mostly. Long pauses, self-corrections that restart the sentence. |
| IM | Strings of 2–3 sentences on familiar topics, but time frames mix and the links are "and / then / so". |
| IH | Confident on familiar topics, handles a complication, but narration stays sentence-by-sentence; past tense appears but is not sustained. |
| AL | **Paragraph-length** answers in **all time frames**, sentences **connected** with transitions, a complication resolved, varied vocabulary. Errors exist but never block the message. |

- **Over-rehearsal warning**: ACTFL explicitly notes that abundant rehearsed language may prevent a
  rating beyond Novice-High. Drill structures and transitions — then generate content live.

## 2. Transitions (전환어) — priority 1

Discourse connectors are a scored AL feature. Learn them by FUNCTION, not as one long list.

### 2.1 Starting an answer

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| Let me just talk about ~ | clean, confident opener — goes straight into the topic |
| The main thing is ~ | signals your main point is coming |
| To keep it short / put simply | buys time, promises structure |
| I'd like to talk about ~ | safe, slightly formal |
| What I wanna say is ~ | casual, natural |
| If I'm honest / Straight up / Let me cut to the chase | confident, casual |
| Here's the bottom line / Here's what really matters | puts the conclusion first |
| Generally speaking / As far as I know | for general-fact questions |
| That's an interesting question, let me think… | stall when you need a second |

### 2.2 Comparison (past ↔ now) — the set that separates IH from AL

Raters listen for **contrast language**, not two separate facts.

- Back then, ~ / **Nowadays**, ~ · **In the past**, ~ / **These days**, ~
- **Compared to the past**, people ~ much more these days.
- **Whereas** before I used to X, **now** I Y.
- **On the other hand**, ~ / By way of contrast, ~
- Things are **completely different** now. / **The biggest difference is** ~.
- Combo: "Back then, I **used to** X. But nowadays, I Y instead."

### 2.3 Past experience (sequencing a story)

- So, this happened **a few years ago**, when I was ~.
- **At first**, ~ → **But then**, out of nowhere, ~ → **In the end**, everything worked out.
- **Something unexpected happened** when I ~ed. / **However, I once had a problem** when I ~ed.
- **Looking back on it now**, I still remember how ~.
- **Because of that experience**, I learned the importance of ~.
- Direct quotation (strong move): "I was like, 'No way!'" / "My friend was like, 'Are you serious?'"

### 2.4 Habit / Description

- **Usually**, I ~, but **every once in a while**, I ~.
- **What I normally do is** ~, and then I ~.
- **Whenever / Wherever** ~, I always ~. · **No matter what** happens, I ~.
- **Most of the time**, I ~ · When I have free time, I ~
- **One of my favorite activities** is ~ · I really enjoy ~ing **in my free time**.

### 2.5 Wrapping up

- **So that's basically** how/why ~ · **Anyway**, that's my routine / how I got into it.
- **Overall**, I really enjoy ~ing. · **Personally**, I find it (relaxing / refreshing).
- **That's why** ~ing has become an important part of my life.
- **For me**, ~ is a great way to relieve stress.

### 2.6 Hard question / stalling

- Wow, **that's quite a tough question**. / I didn't expect such a hard question.
- **Let me think for a second…** / How should I put this…
- **The thing is**, it really depends on the situation.
- **As far as I know**, ~ / From what I know, ~

### 2.7 Role-play frames (RP11–13)

- Purpose first: **I'm calling because** I have a question about ~ / **I'd like to ask about** ~
- Request: **Is it possible to** ~? / **Could you** ~? / **I was wondering if** ~ is available.
- Obligation: **Do I need to** ~? / **Should I** ~?
- Problem: **I have a problem with my** ~ / **Something went wrong with** my ~.
- Thanks / close: **I really appreciate your help with** my ~ · **I'm glad we solved the** ~ issue.

### 2.8 Referring back inside a combo set (콤보 연결)

Combo questions are RELATED — referring back to an earlier answer is cheap discourse control and the
raters reward it.

- **As I mentioned before**, ~
- **I think I mentioned this already**, but ~
- **Like I said earlier / in the previous question**, ~
- **Going back to what I said before**, ~
- ★ **To build on what I just said**, ~
- ★ **The same thing happened when** ~ (bridges a PE question to the previous answer)

## 3. Tense (시제) — priority 2

Two rules cover most errors.

- **Specific past time ⇒ simple past, never present perfect.**
  ❌ I have visited Busan last summer. → ✅ I **visited** Busan last summer.
  Present perfect only with no specific time: "I've been to Busan a few times."
- **`used to` = a repeated past habit that stopped** — this IS the Comparison answer:
  "I **used to** X. **But these days**, I Y." / "I don't X **as often as** I used to."

Reading topic, tense-fixed as a model:

> I **didn't use to** read when I was younger — I **preferred** watching TV and I **wasted** too much time
> on it. **But these days**, I **read** almost every day, and I **joined** a reading club last year.

**Narration drill**: anchor = **simple past** for the storyline. Shift only twice — **past perfect** for
something before the story ("By the time I got there, it had already closed") and **present** for the
closing reflection ("Now, whenever I ~, I laugh"). Record one story, listen for tense slips only,
re-record. Never restart on a slip — note it, keep talking.

## 4. Plurals (복수형) — priority 3

Korean has no plural marking, so this is the highest-frequency **pattern** error. Four templates:

1. **one of the + PLURAL + singular verb**: "One of the **things** I like most **is** ~" / "One of my **friends** lives in Busan."
2. **many / most / several / a few / a couple of + PLURAL**: "Most **people** nowadays ~" / "a couple of **close friends**."
3. **I like / enjoy + PLURAL category**: "I like **action movies**" — mass nouns stay singular: "I enjoy **music**."
4. **Irregulars, no extra -s**: children, people, men, women, feet, teeth.

**Uncountable traps** (never add -s; use "a piece of / an item of" to count):

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| furnitures, advices | furniture, advice — "a piece of furniture / a piece of advice" |
| informations, softwares | information, software |
| equipments, luggages | equipment, luggage — "a piece of equipment / a bag of luggage" |
| homeworks, newses | homework, news — always singular |

**Spoken self-check**: after any quantity word (many, most, several, a few, two…), pronounce the -s
deliberately. Fix it first on the high-frequency templates — that is where the pattern shows.

## 5. Articles (관사) — priority 4

- **First mention = a/an → every mention after = the.** The narrative rule:
  "I went to **a** cafe near my house. **The** cafe was sleek, and **the** barista was friendly."
- **the** for unique / context-known things: **the** subway, **the** bus, **the** weather, **the** internet,
  **the** gym (the one you go to), **the** park near my house.
- **Zero article** for plural/uncountable generalities: "I like **books**." / "I listen to **music**."
- Korean-speaker omission to fix actively: ~~I have dog~~ → I have **a** dog. ~~I went to gym~~ → I went to **the** gym.

## 6. Other pattern risks (he/she · prepositions)

- **he / she**: Korean pronouns are gender-neutral, so mixing them mid-story is a classic Korean-L1
  pattern error. When telling a PE about a person, decide the gender in the first sentence and keep it.
- **in / on / at**: **in** the morning / **in** Korea · **on** Monday / **on** the weekend · **at** night / **at** 7 p.m.
- **discuss** takes no "about": "we discussed **the plan**" (✗ discussed about the plan).
- **agree with** a person, **agree on** a plan.

## 7. Question playbooks

### 7.1 Description / Habit (Q1 of a combo, ~1 min — keep it SHORT, low weight)

- Opener → what/where/when → **feeling + why** → wrap.
- "To keep this answer short, I'll focus on ~."
- Habit connector: "**Whenever** I have free time, I ~" + "**That's why** I enjoy ~ing so much."
- Include at least one of: What / Feeling / Why.

### 7.2 Past Experience (up to 1:30 — HIGH weight)

- MP → BODY (story) → CONCLUSION, **with a direct quotation**.
- Skeleton: "I remember when I ~ed." → "At first ~. But then, something unexpected happened —" →
  quotation → "In the end, ~. Because of that experience, I learned ~."
- Put yourself with family/friends in the story — the quotation writes itself.
- Complication vocabulary: something went wrong / out of nowhere / I panicked / it turned out / it worked out.

### 7.3 Comparison (up to 1:30 — HIGH weight)

- **Contrast language only** (see 2.2). MP = now → Past = the opposite → back to MP.
- Skeleton: "These days, I ~. **Back then**, I **used to** ~ — the complete opposite. **The biggest
  difference is** ~. **Overall**, I prefer how things are now **because** ~."

### 7.4 Role play (RP11–13)

- **RP11** (survey/message): identify the channel (voice message / call / in person), ask the required
  number of questions, **imagine the other side's answers out loud**, close naturally.
- **RP12** (two-question problem): Q1 → imagine "No, unfortunately ~" → pivot: "Then could I at least ~?"
  → imagine "Yes, of course." → thank.
- **RP13** (problem/solution PE): don't wait to be asked — the problem is your MP, then the solution experience.

### 7.5 Combo strategy (Set 2 / Set 3)

- Set 2: Description → Comparison → PE. **2nd question = Comparison ⇒ 3rd is confirmed Comparison.**
- Set 3: Description → PE → PE/Comparison. **2nd = PE ⇒ 3rd is undecided.**
- Description/Habit under 1 minute scores low; PE/Comparison up to 1:30 scores high — spend the time there.
- Use 2.8 (callbacks) in questions 2–3: "Like I said earlier, ~" — free discourse points.

## 8. Q1 self-introduction model

The self-introduction always comes first (and is skipped in every mock video) — prepare it cold:

> Hi, my name is ~. I'm a ~ (software engineer) at a company that develops **robotics and automation
> software**. There are **about ~ people** on my team, and I've been working there for **~ years**.
> **Outside of work**, I love reading — I'm actually **in a reading club** that meets twice a month.
> **These days** I'm also trying to **stay in shape**, so I work out a few times a week.
> **Anyway**, I'm looking forward to this interview — let's get started.

Structure: name → job (+ one detail) → one hobby with a **these-days** frame → hand-off. 30–40 seconds.
Say one concrete number and one **but/these days** — it opens the door to every later question on YOUR terms.

## 9. Adjective upgrades ("varied adjectives" is an AL marker)

| Weak | Upgrade |
|---|---|
| very good | fantastic / awesome / amazing |
| very bad | terrible / awful |
| very big / small | huge / tiny |
| very tired | exhausted / worn out |
| very happy | thrilled / delighted |
| very interesting | fascinating |
| very cold / hot | freezing / boiling |
| very surprised | stunned |
| very funny | hilarious |
| very important | crucial / essential |
| very pretty | gorgeous |

Intensifiers from the notes: **really / so / very / absolutely** — "I **absolutely** love it" /
"It's **so** refreshing" / "like **a lot**" — and the strong-adjective compliments: fantastic, amazing,
spectacular, wonderful, awesome.

## 10. Survival kit (blanking out · unknown words)

- **Buying time**: Let me think for a second… / How should I put this… / That's a tough one.
- **Unknown word → paraphrase**: "It's **a thing you use to** ~" / "It's **like a** ~, **but for** ~" /
  "I can't remember the exact word, but it's **something that** ~".
- **Recovering mid-sentence**: "…**anyway, what I wanted to say was** ~" / "…**or something like that**."
- **Wrong tense halfway**: finish the sentence, put the NEXT sentence back in the anchor tense. Do not restart.
- **Nothing to say**: switch to feeling + reason ("**Personally**, I find it ~ **because** ~") — a
  feeling sentence is always available, for any topic.

## 11. Topic cards (grammar-checked lines)

| Topic | Core lines | Grammar to hit |
|---|---|---|
| Cafe (Starbucks) | "It's a big 4-story building. The first floor is a little noisy but lively, full of energy. The higher floors are quiet, so many students study there. It's sleek with modern lighting. The Vanilla Cream Cold Brew is low in sugar and calories." | a cafe → the cafe → the first floor; plural (drinks, students) |
| Coffee / drinks | "I usually choose my drink depending on my mood. I often order a Frapuccino — very sweet, quite popular in Korea. The Vanilla Cream Cold Brew is also sweet, but not as sweet, and it has **fewer** calories and **less** sugar. So I enjoy both, but I pick depending on my mood." | fewer vs less (countable/uncountable) |
| Reading / hobby | "I didn't use to read when I was younger — I preferred TV. But these days I read almost every day. Bookstores have a warm atmosphere; libraries are always quiet. I joined a reading club — at first it felt unfamiliar, but now I become the main character." | used to / didn't use to; a→the |
| Gym | "I work out at least three times a week to stay in shape. I was a little overweight before I started. The gym I go to has a lot of equipment. First I stretch so I won't get hurt; after running, I do weight training to build muscle." | habit connectors; equipment (uncountable) |
| Transportation | "I love how I can read something on public transit. It's the only time I can actually read — interesting, because I never liked reading before." | the + transit nouns; present habit |
| Recycling | "It's really rare for me to forget to check the recycling. I'm meticulous about it now — interesting, because I didn't recycle often before." | didn't use to; the + recycling |
| Furniture | "To keep this answer short, I'll focus on **one piece of furniture** I love: in the living room, there's a big wooden table where I read. My apartment is small but cozy." | a→the; furniture (uncountable) |
| Neighborhood | "When I first moved in, everything was brand new. There's a great cafe not far from where I live." | a→the; moved in |
| Music | "I like pop, especially American pop, because it cheers me up. I often go to classical concerts — symphonies, piano recitals. I love his music because he composes great melodies and lyrics; most of his songs are popular in Korea." | plural categories; he (keep the gender consistent) |
| Movies | "I usually go to the theater in my neighborhood. It's small, with a few screenings, so it's mostly not crowded — I can always watch quietly whenever I want. I'd heard pretty good reviews about the film." | the theater (known); past perfect (had heard) |
| Park | "I take a walk on the walking path. It takes about 30 minutes around the whole park, so I do a few laps. I put on sunblock and sometimes spend a whole afternoon there." | the + park features; a few laps |
| Travel (China PE) | "It was my first school trip outside Korea, so I made my first passport. We used public transportation to get around. The food was traditional Chinese — oily but delicious. I gained weight, and it's not easy to lose, so I never miss my workout now." | simple past throughout; because-conclusion |
| Trip plan | "I would most likely go to Jeju for a domestic trip. I always try to pack light — a heavy suitcase is a big hassle. I also pack an emergency medical kit. When I get there, I'd like to photograph the ancient architecture." | would/conditional; pack light |
| Climate | "When it gets really cold in Korea, the water pipes can freeze." | the + unique things |
| Technology | "My phone is pretty old but still very usable. One advantage of technology is convenience — our lives have become much more convenient. However, some people worry about addiction. In the future, I think it will become even more common." | advantage/disadvantage frame; future will |

## 12. Final checklist

**Do**

- Open with a transition, close with a feeling + "That's why ~".
- Keep PE / Comparison to 60–90+ seconds; Description/Habit short.
- One anchor tense per story; `used to` for every past-vs-now contrast.
- `a` on first mention, `the` after; -s after every quantity word.
- Quote someone in every PE ("I was like, ~").
- Refer back inside combos ("Like I said earlier, ~").
- If a run wedges: finish the sentence, put the next one back on track. Recovery, not perfection.

**Don't**

- Don't recite a memorized script word-for-word (it caps the rating).
- Don't answer a Comparison with two unrelated facts — contrast them.
- Don't say "one of my friend" / "I like action movie" / "I went to gym".
- Don't leave a silence: fill it with "Let me think for a second…" — fillers are strategy, not errors.
