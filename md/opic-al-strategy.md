# OPIc AL Strategy Guide (문법·전략 총정리)

Target: **AL (Advanced Low)**. This guide is built from personal notes + the ACTFL Advanced-Low descriptor.

**How to use it:** every case file has a **Backbone** — your own sentences assembled into one complete
spoken answer, labeled by role. Shadow the backbone for structure — then answer live with new content. Everything left over is a **Phrase
Bank** — reusable pieces for other questions. Case-specific **Watch-points** flag the grammar the raters
will hear in THAT answer. Do not recite scripts word-for-word; drill the structures, generate content live.

## 1. How OPIc scoring works (시험 구조와 채점)

- ~40-minute recorded computer interview; questions come from your **Background Survey**.
- Levels: IL → IM (1–3) → **IH → AL**. The IH→AL jump is the goal.
- **The AL descriptor, concretely**: narrate/describe in **all major time frames** (past · present ·
  future) in **paragraph-length discourse**; **connect sentences with conjunctions so they flow as one
  discourse**; handle a **complication** (unexpected turn) in a familiar situation. AL vocabulary tip:
  **varied adjectives** (see §10).
- **"Errors do not interfere"** = patterns matter, not slips. One dropped plural **-s** is nothing;
  dropping it on every countable noun is a pattern — patterns block AL.

| Level | What the rater hears |
|---|---|
| IL | Short sentences, one at a time. Present tense mostly. Long pauses, restarts. |
| IM | Strings of 2–3 sentences on familiar topics, but time frames mix and the links are "and / then / so". |
| IH | Confident on familiar topics and routine situations; a complication can still cause breakdown. Narration connects, but is not fully sustained. |
| AL | **Paragraph-length** answers in **all time frames**, sentences **connected** with transitions, a complication resolved, varied vocabulary. Errors exist but never block the message. |

### Combo structure (콤보 구조)

- Questions come in sets of 3 (after the self-intro). **PE = Past Experience · RP = Role Play.**
- **Set 2**: Description → Comparison → PE. **2nd = Comparison ⇒ Set 2 confirmed: 3rd = PE.**
- **Set 3**: Description → PE → PE/Comparison. **2nd = PE ⇒ 3rd undecided.**
- Description/Habit: aim ~60 s. **PE/Comparison: aim 60–90 s** — more time to show the AL features.
- When a question arrives: identify the **category** first, then pick your **MP (main point)**.
  MP ≈ 30 seconds: **What + Feeling + Why**. Habit answers include at least one of the three.
- Housing/Home and first-question Description/Habit come up often — prepare them cold **if you selected them in the survey**.
- **RP11** (survey, 3–4 questions): know the channel (voice message / phone call / in person). On a
  call, imagine the other side's replies and respond; on a voicemail, leave all your questions in one message.
- **RP12** (complication): explain the problem clearly, then offer alternatives — ask Q1 → imagine
  "**No, unfortunately ~**" → pivot "Then could I at least ~?" → imagine "**Yes, of course.**" → thank.
- **RP13** (problem/solution PE): don't wait to be asked — the problem is your MP, then the solution experience.
- **IHU14/15** (final survey block): 14 = Comparison (past↔now, or A/B) · 15 = Society / people questions.
- PE feels real if you went **with family or friends** — that gives you the quotation for free.
- Over-rehearsal caps the rating (ACTFL): drill structures, generate content live.

## 2. Transitions (전환어) — priority 1

Connectors are a scored AL feature. Learn them by FUNCTION. **[core]** = memorize first · **[extra]** = use
when it fits. Pick 2–3 per function and use them in every answer until they are automatic.
Written-register words (thus, therefore, in addition…) — see 2.17.

### 2.1 Starting

| Phrase | Note |
|---|---|
| **Let me just talk about** ~ | clean, confident opener |
| **The main thing is** ~ | signals the main point |
| **To keep it short / put simply** | buys time, promises structure |
| **I'd like to talk about** ~ | safe, slightly formal |
| **What I wanna say is** ~ | casual, natural |
| **If I'm honest / Straight up** | confident, casual |
| **Let me cut to the chase** | works best mid-answer, after some context |
| **Here's the bottom line / I'll be upfront** | conclusion first |
| **Generally speaking** | general-fact questions |
| **That's an interesting question, let me think…** | stall when you need a second |

### 2.2 Contrast: past ↔ now (Comparison questions)

- Back then, ~ / **Nowadays**, ~ · **In the past** / **Back in the day** → **These days** / **Nowadays**
- **Compared to the past**, people ~ much more these days.
- **However**, ~ · Things are **completely different** now. / **The biggest difference is** ~.

### 2.3 Habit / Description

- **Usually**, I ~, but **every once in a while**, I ~.
- **What I normally do is** ~ · **Most of the time**, I ~ · When I have free time, I ~
- **Whenever / Wherever** ~, I always ~ · **No matter what** happens, I ~ · **Every time** ~, I ~

### 2.4 Past experience (sequencing a story)

- So, this happened **a few years ago**, when I was ~.
- **At first**, ~ → **But then**, out of nowhere, ~ → **In the end**, everything worked out.
- **Looking back on it now**, I still remember how ~.
- **He/I was like, "~"** — the quotation move for PE.

### 2.5 Wrapping up

- **So that's basically** how/why ~ · **Anyway**, that's my routine.
- **Overall**, I really enjoy ~ing. · **Personally**, I find it relaxing.
- **That's why** I enjoy ~ing so much. / **That's why** ~ing has become an important part of my life.
- **For me**, ~ is a great way to relax.

### 2.6 Referring back inside a combo set (콤보 연결)

Combo questions are RELATED — a callback shows discourse control. Use it when it fits naturally.

- **As I mentioned before**, ~
- **I think I mentioned this already**, but ~
- **Like I said earlier / in the previous question**, ~
- **Going back to what I said before**, ~
- **To build on what I just said**, ~
- **The same thing happened when** ~

### 2.7 Hard question / stalling

- **Wow**, that's quite a tough question. / I didn't expect such a hard question.
- **Let me think for a second…** / How should I put this…
- **As far as I know**, ~ (when you're not sure)

### 2.8 돌발 (unexpected) / framing

- **To tell you the truth**, ~ · **Here in Korea**, ~ · **People often say that** ~
- **The bottom line is that** ~ · **This is crucial** because ~
- **The good/best thing is that** ~ · **The reason is/was that** ~ (= because)

### 2.17 Formal words — thus, therefore, hence, moreover, nevertheless

These are correct but sound **essay-like spoken out loud**. Use sparingly — the spoken alternative is
easier to say and scores the same.

| Formal | Spoken replacement | Example (formal) |
|---|---|---|
| thus | so / that's why | The subway was delayed, and thus I arrived late at the cafe. |
| therefore | so / that's why | The gym was closed; therefore, I went for a walk instead. |
| hence | so | I missed my bus, so I had a long walk home. |
| moreover | on top of that | The cafe is quiet; moreover, it's close to my apartment. |
| nevertheless | still / even so | It rained during our Jeju trip; nevertheless, we had a great time. |

## 3. Tense (시제) — priority 2

- **Specific past time ⇒ simple past**, never present perfect.
  X: I have visited Busan last summer. → O: I **visited** Busan last summer.
  Present perfect for no specific time OR duration: "I've been to Busan a few times." / "I've worked here **for** five years."
- **`used to` = a repeated past habit that stopped** — the Comparison answer:
  "I **used to** X. **But these days**, I Y." / "I don't X **as often as** I used to."
- **Narration drill**: anchor = **simple past**. Shift only twice — past perfect for before-the-story
  ("By the time I got there, it had already closed") and present for the closing reflection
  ("Now, whenever I ~, I laugh"). Record one story; listen for tense slips only; re-record.
  Never restart on a slip — note it, keep talking.

## 4. Plurals (복수형) — priority 3

Four templates cover most spoken cases:

1. **one of the + PLURAL + singular verb**: "One of the **things** I like most **is** ~" / "One of my **friends** lives in Busan."
2. **many / most / several / a few / a couple of + PLURAL**: "Many **students** study there."
3. **I like / enjoy + PLURAL category**: "I like **action movies**" — mass nouns stay singular: "I enjoy **music**."
4. **Irregulars, no extra -s**: children, people, men, women, feet, teeth.

**Uncountable traps** (no -s; count with "a piece of"):

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| furnitures, advices | furniture, advice — a piece of furniture / advice |
| informations, softwares | information, software |
| equipments, luggages | equipment, luggage |
| homeworks, newses | homework, news |

Self-check: after any quantity word, pronounce the **-s** deliberately.

## 5. Articles (관사) — priority 4

- **First mention = a/an → every mention after = the**: "I went to **a** cafe near my house. **The**
  cafe was sleek, and **the** barista was friendly."
- **the** for unique/context-known: **the** subway, **the** bus, **the** weather, **the** internet,
  **the** gym (the one you go to), **the** park near my house.
- **Zero article** for plural/uncountable generalities: "I like **books**." / "I listen to **music**."
- Fix actively: "I have **a** dog." / "I went to **the** gym." (Korean drops these.)
- **the** also when the context makes it obvious: "I went to **a** cafe. **The** barista was friendly."

## 6. Other pattern risks

- **he / she**: Korean pronouns are gender-neutral — decide the person's gender in the first sentence of
  a story and keep it. Mixing mid-story is a classic Korean-L1 pattern error.
- **in / on / at**: **in** the morning / **in** Korea · **on** Monday / **on** the weekend · **at** night / **at** 7 p.m.
- **discuss** takes no "about": "we discussed **the plan**". · **agree with** a person, **agree on** a plan.
- **interested in** (~에 관심 있는) vs **interesting** (~흥미로운) — people ARE interested; things ARE interesting.

## 7. 말 늘리기 — the MP framework (expanding any answer)

| Move | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **MP · Current** | What is it + **Feeling** (like/dislike) + **Reason** (The reason is that ~ / because ~) |
| **Past (opposite)** | In the past, I ~ / Back in the day, I used to ~ (say the OPPOSITE of your MP) |
| **Current again** | **However**, ~ / **These days**, ~ / Nowadays, ~ |

Fillers (in moderation — clusters of hesitation count as disfluency): **well / you know / I mean / let me see…**
Quotes for PE: **he/I was like, "~"**.
Frames: **The good/best thing is that ~** · **the reason is/was that ~** · **People often say that** ~ ·
**This is crucial** because ~ · **Could/Would you please** ~ (requests).

## 8. Case files (케이스별 모범 답안)

Each case: **Target question → Backbone ([B], shadow as one answer) → Phrase bank ([P], reusable) →
Watch-points → Past variation → Related questions.** Your own sentences, in speaking order.

### 8.1 음료 — Favorite drink

**Target:** "What drinks do you enjoy? How do you choose?"

**Backbone**

1. **[B · Opener]** I usually choose my drink depending on my mood.
2. **[B · MP]** I often order a Frappuccino.
3. **[B · Detail]** It's a very sweet drink, and it's quite popular in Korea.
4. **[B · Feeling]** Personally, I like it because it's good for relaxing, especially when I'm stressed from work.
5. **[B · MP]** Another drink I like is **the** Vanilla Cream Cold Brew.
6. **[B · Contrast]** It's also sweet, but it's not as sweet as a Frappuccino.
7. **[B · Detail]** And it has less sugar and fewer calories.
8. **[B · Wrap]** So, I enjoy both drinks, but I choose between them depending on my mood.

**Phrase bank**

- **[P · Frame]** I usually choose ___ depending on my mood.
- **[P · Feeling]** I like it because it's good for relaxing.
- **[P · Contrast]** It's sweet, but it's not as sweet as ___.
- **[P · Comparison]** It has less sugar and fewer calories.

**Watch-points**

- **less sugar** (uncountable) vs **fewer calories** (countable plural) — you already have this right; keep it.
- **not as sweet as** — the comparison pattern for two things.

**Past variation** (your notes don't say what you drank before — fill this in before shadowing):
"In the past, I usually ordered ___. These days, I order a Frappuccino or Vanilla Cream Cold Brew, depending on my mood."

**Related questions:** "Describe a drink you often order." (backbone 2–4) · "Compare two drinks you
like." (5–8) · "What do you order when you're stressed?" (4 + a personal example).

**Backbone**

1. **[B · Opener]** There is a great cafe near my place that I go to all the time — a Starbucks.
2. **[B · Detail]** It's a big four-story building.
3. **[B · Detail]** The first floor is a little bit noisy, but it's lively and full of energy.
4. **[B · Detail]** The higher floors are quiet, so there are many students there.

6. **[B · Detail]** I love their food and drinks — a lot of people in Korea love Frappuccinos.
7. **[B · Detail]** **Their** Vanilla Cream Cold Brew is low in sugar and low in calories.
8. **[B · MP]** I love reading books there — that's why I go.

**Phrase bank**

- **[P · Frame]** There is a great ___ near my place that I go to.
- **[P · Detail]** It's located not far from where I live.
- **[P · Feeling]** I love reading books there.

**Watch-points**

- **a** cafe (first mention) → **the** first floor / **the** higher floors (specific parts of that known building).
- "a **four-story** building" — hyphenated when it describes a noun.

**Related questions:** "Describe your neighborhood." (the opener + location lines) · "Where do you read
books?" (wrap + the reading case below).

### 8.3 취미/독서 — Reading

**Target:** "Tell me about a hobby you enjoy."

**Backbone**

1. **[B · Opener]** I often read books as a hobby — I love books.
2. **[B · Detail]** I always concentrate when I read books.
3. **[B · Detail]** I get immersed in the story — I always feel like I become the main character when I read novels.
4. **[B · Detail]** As for where I like to read — bookstores have a warm atmosphere, and libraries are always quiet and friendly when I go there.
5. **[B · Detail]** On top of that, I'm in a reading club.
7. **[B · Contrast]** Joining the reading club was easy, but at first, it felt unfamiliar.

## 9. Q1 self-introduction model

The self-introduction always comes first (the mock-exam videos skip it — drill it here):

> Hi, my name is ___. I'm a software engineer at a company that develops robotics and automation
> software. I've been working there for ___ years. **Outside of work**, I love reading — I'm actually
> in a reading club that meets twice a month. **These days** I'm also trying to **stay in shape**, so I work
> out a few times a week. Anyway, I'm looking forward to this interview — let's get started.

Structure: name → job (+ one detail) → two hooks (reading + gym) → hand-off. 30–40 seconds.
It previews your survey topics and sets an AL tone right away.

## 10. Adjective upgrades ("varied adjectives" is an AL marker)

| Weak | Upgrade |
|---|---|
| very good | fantastic / awesome / amazing |
| very bad | terrible / awful |
| very big / small | huge / tiny |
| very tired | exhausted / worn out |
| very happy | thrilled / delighted |
| very interesting | fascinating |
| very cold / hot | freezing / boiling |
| very surprised | stunned |
| very funny | hilarious |
| very important | crucial / essential |
| very pretty | gorgeous |

Intensifiers: **really / so / absolutely** — "I **absolutely** love it". ⚠ **very** cannot upgrade extreme
adjectives: ~~very exhausted~~ → **absolutely exhausted**, ~~very freezing~~ → **freezing**.
Quantity: **a lot of ~ / a bunch of ~ / like a lot**.
Praise adjectives: **fantastic / amazing / spectacular / wonderful / awesome**.

## 11. Survival kit (blanking out · unknown words)

- **Buying time**: Let me think for a second… / How should I put this… / That's a tough one.
- **Unknown word → paraphrase**: "It's **a thing you use to** ~" / "It's **like a** ~, **but for** ~" /
  "I can't remember the exact word, but it's **something that** ~".
- **Recovering mid-sentence**: "…**anyway, what I wanted to say was** ~" / "…**or something like that**."
- **Wrong tense halfway**: finish the sentence, put the NEXT sentence back in the anchor tense. Do not restart.
- **Nothing to say**: switch to feeling + reason — "**Personally**, I find it ~ **because** ~" — a feeling
  sentence is always available, for any topic.

## 12. Final checklist

**Do**

- Open with a transition, close with a feeling + "That's why ~".
- Keep PE / Comparison around 60–90 s; Description/Habit short.
- One anchor tense per story; `used to` for every past-vs-now contrast.
- `a` on first mention, `the` after; -s after **many / most / several / a few / a couple of**.
- Quote someone in every PE ("I was like, ~") — and go with family/friends in the story.
- Refer back inside combos ("Like I said earlier, ~").
- If a run wedges: finish the sentence, put the next one back on track. Recovery, not perfection.

**Don't**

- Don't recite a memorized script word-for-word (it caps the rating).
- Don't answer a Comparison with two unrelated facts — contrast them.
- Don't say "one of my friend" / "I like action movie" / "I went to gym" / "furnitures".
- Don't leave a silence — fill it with "Let me think for a second…". Fillers are strategy, in moderation.
